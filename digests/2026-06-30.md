### CLUSTER 1 — Constitutional & Law Enforcement

**SCOTUSblog / NPR — June 29, 2026** [PIPELINE HIT]


The Supreme Court ruled that police officers conducted a Fourth Amendment search when they acquired Okello Chatrie's location data from Google, affirming that an individual has a reasonable expectation of privacy in cell-phone location information.

 The 6-3 majority remanded to a lower court to determine whether the geofence search was "reasonable." This directly narrows the surveillance toolkit available to federal law enforcement.

**SCOTUSblog / SCOTUS Alerts — June 25, 2026** [PIPELINE HIT]


The Supreme Court held that the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act expressly preempts state-law failure-to-warn claims, because such claims would require Monsanto to add a cancer warning to its Roundup products' label

 — a significant federal preemption win with downstream implications for regulatory tort litigation.

**SCOTUS Alerts — June 23, 2026**


The Court held that the proper baseline for measuring "just compensation" following a tax sale is the auction sale price, not the property's hypothetical fair market value, and that Isabella County did not violate the Eighth Amendment Excessive Fines Clause.

 This eminent domain/forfeiture ruling limits property rights remedies in tax-sale disputes.

**SCOTUS Alerts — June 30, 2026** [PIPELINE HIT]


The Court ruled that children born in the United States to parents unlawfully or temporarily present are citizens under the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship Clause; separately, the Court struck down Federal Election Campaign Act restrictions on party spending coordinated with candidates as a First Amendment violation.

**Stars and Stripes / NGAUS — April 2026 (FY2027 Budget in Congress Now)**


The Pentagon formally unveiled its $1.5 trillion budget for fiscal 2027, calling for raises of 5% to 7% for troops, an increase of 44,000 personnel in the overall force, and spending $750 billion on defense capabilities and weapons systems.

### CLUSTER 3 — National Debt & Fiscal Policy [PIPELINE HIT]

**SSA Board of Trustees / CRFB / Bipartisan Policy Center — June 9, 2026**


The 2026 Trustees Report projects the OASI Trust Fund will pay 100% of scheduled benefits only until Q4 2032 — one quarter earlier than last year — at which point reserves will be depleted and continuing income will cover only 78% of benefits.

 

The Medicare Hospital Insurance fund is similarly projected for depletion in Q2 2033, also one quarter earlier than last year's report.



**Bipartisan Policy Center / CRFB — June 9, 2026** [PIPELINE HIT]


The accelerated Social Security depletion date is largely due to the 2025 "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," which included provisions lowering tax liability for Social Security beneficiaries, reducing projected trust fund revenue going forward.

 

Social Security's 75-year solvency gap grew 16% relative to last year's projections; over half the deterioration stems from lower projected fertility rates, a third from lower assumed immigration, and a quarter from the OBBBA's reduction of revenue from income taxation of benefits.
