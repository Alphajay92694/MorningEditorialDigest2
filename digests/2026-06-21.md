### CLUSTER 3 — National Debt & Fiscal Policy **[PIPELINE HIT]**

**CNBC / Bipartisan Policy Center — June 9, 2026**


The Social Security Administration's annual trustees report found that the retirement trust fund may run out in late 2032 — three months earlier than last year's projection — and that if depleted, Social Security will only be able to pay 78% of retirement benefits.

 

The new projected depletion date follows the enactment of President Trump's "big beautiful" tax law, which Social Security's chief actuary said would have "material effects" on trust fund finances.



**Bipartisan Policy Center / AARP — June 9, 2026**


The 2026 Medicare Trustees report, released June 9, concludes that Medicare remains on an unsustainable course, projecting that the Medicare Hospital Insurance Part A Trust Fund will be depleted in 2033.

 

Medicare insolvency would lead to an automatic 11% cut in payments, undermining access to care.



**Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget — June 9, 2026**


Thanks mainly to tax cuts in the "One Big Beautiful Bill" and worsening demographics, Social Security's projected shortfall is a full 16% worse than last year's, and Medicare's shortfall is 33% worse.



**CBO / Cato Institute — February 2026**


Net interest — the cost of servicing the national debt — will double from $970 billion in 2025 to $2.1 trillion in 2036; by 2036, interest payments will nearly equal all discretionary spending combined.
