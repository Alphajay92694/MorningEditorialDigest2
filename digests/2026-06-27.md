### CLUSTER 1 — Constitutional & Law Enforcement

**SCOTUSblog / January 16, 2026 (current term)**


The Supreme Court this term agreed to hear *Chatrie v. United States*, a Fourth Amendment challenge to the constitutionality of geofence warrants — orders that allow law enforcement to obtain the identities of cell phone users present in a specific area at a specific time.



**Congress.gov / CRS — February 12, 2026**


In *Case v. Montana* (January 14, 2026), the Supreme Court unanimously resolved a longstanding circuit split, holding that law enforcement need not possess probable cause to enter a home without a warrant to render emergency aid, reasoning that probable cause applies to criminal investigative functions — not public safety responsibilities.



**SCOTUS Watch — June 9, 2026 (pending cert)**

### CLUSTER 2 — Military & National Security

**Stars and Stripes / April 21, 2026**

### CLUSTER 3 — National Debt & Fiscal Policy [PIPELINE HIT]

**Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget — June 9, 2026**


The 2026 Social Security and Medicare Trustees' reports project that the Social Security OASI trust fund will be insolvent in 2032 (or 2034 with disability fund reallocation), while Medicare's Hospital Insurance trust fund will deplete its reserves by 2033.



**American Action Forum — June 9, 2026** [PIPELINE HIT]


The Social Security Trustees now estimate the OASI trust fund will exhaust its reserves by 2032, one year earlier than last year's projection.

 

The Trustees significantly reduced their fertility and immigration assumptions, reflecting recent demographic trends and policy changes, thereby lowering projected future payroll tax revenues.



**CBO / Cato Institute — February 2026**


Federal debt held by the public is projected to reach 101% of GDP in 2026; by 2030, it will surpass the World War II high of 106%, ballooning to 120% by 2036 and 175% by 2056.

 

Autopilot mandatory spending — interest costs and programs like Social Security and Medicare — is almost entirely responsible for this growth; by 2036, Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, and interest costs will account for 73% of total federal spending, consuming nearly 100% of all federal revenue.



**House Budget Committee Chairman Arrington — March 3, 2026**


Arrington stated that "the structural imbalance in our federal budget — driven primarily by autopilot spending in our largest entitlement programs — cannot be ignored any longer," and announced the House Budget Committee will pursue a bipartisan debt commission.
