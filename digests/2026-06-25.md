### CLUSTER 1 — Constitutional & Law Enforcement

**Supreme Court / scotusalerts.com — June 25, 2026** [PIPELINE HIT]


The Supreme Court ruled today in *Wolford v. Lopez* that Hawaii's law prohibiting licensed concealed-carry permit holders from carrying handguns on private property open to the public without the property owner's express authorization violates the Second and Fourteenth Amendments.

 

Hawaii had replaced its prior near-total bar on carry permits with new laws that achieved a similar restrictive result, including a blanket prohibition on firearms on private property open to the public absent affirmative owner consent.

 Decision authored by Justice Alito, joined by Roberts, Thomas, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett; Kagan and Jackson dissented.

**Supreme Court / scotusalerts.com — June 25, 2026** [PIPELINE HIT]


The Court also ruled today in *Monsanto Co. v. Durnell* that the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act expressly preempts state-law failure-to-warn claims that would require Monsanto to add a cancer warning to its Roundup products' labels

 — a significant Commerce Clause–adjacent federal preemption ruling with implications for state regulatory autonomy over product liability.

**Supreme Court / scotusalerts.com — June 23, 2026**


The Court ruled on June 23 that the proper baseline for measuring "just compensation" following a tax sale is the auction sale price, not the property's hypothetical fair market value, and that Isabella County did not violate the Eighth Amendment Excessive Fines Clause by failing to compensate the taxpayer for fair market value

 — a ruling that narrows property-owner protections in government-compelled sales.

### CLUSTER 3 — National Debt & Fiscal Policy

**SSA Trustees / CRFB — June 9, 2026** [PIPELINE HIT]


The Trustees for Social Security and Medicare released their annual reports showing significant deterioration in both programs: the OASI trust fund is projected to be insolvent in 2032, and Medicare Hospital Insurance faces insolvency in 2033.

 

The accelerated depletion date is largely attributable to the 2025 "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," which lowered tax liability for Social Security beneficiaries and thereby reduced projected trust fund revenue going forward.



**Bipartisan Policy Center — June 9, 2026** [PIPELINE HIT]


The Social Security Administration projected that Social Security's primary trust fund will be depleted in 2032 — a year earlier than last year — with lower projected revenue from income taxes on Social Security benefits stemming from the 2025 reconciliation bill as a major contributor.



**CBO / Congressional Budget Office — February 2026 (current baseline)**


CBO projects the federal budget deficit in fiscal year 2026 at $1.9 trillion, with federal debt rising to 120 percent of GDP by 2036.

 

The 2025 reconciliation act increases CBO's deficit projections by $4.7 trillion over ten years; higher tariffs reduce deficits by approximately $3 trillion; and lower immigration increases deficits by $0.5 trillion.



**House Budget Committee / Chairman Arrington — March 3, 2026**


Interest spending on the national debt is projected to grow from 3.3 percent of GDP in 2026 to 6.9 percent by 2056, and interest payments will surpass total discretionary spending by 2038.
